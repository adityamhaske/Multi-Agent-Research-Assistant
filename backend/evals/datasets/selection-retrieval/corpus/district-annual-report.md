# District annual report

## Enrolment

Enrolment in the North district reached 1,240 pupils in September, the highest figure the district has recorded. Most of the growth came from two new housing estates on the northern ring road. Class sizes in the first two years rose by an average of three pupils. The admissions office processed applications in six weeks rather than the usual nine. ## Attendance

Attendance across the district fell from 95.1 percent to 92.4 percent over the second term. The fall was concentrated in the weeks after the winter break and among the oldest year groups. Schools that called families on the first day of absence recovered attendance fastest. Persistent absence, defined as missing a tenth of sessions, affected one pupil in nine. ## Staffing

The district hired 38 new teachers this year, 22 of them in mathematics and science. Vacancies that stayed open longer than a term fell from fourteen to five. Two schools shared a specialist music teacher for the first time. Teaching assistants received a six-day training course on reading support. ## Transport

A new bus route linking the eastern villages to the secondary school began operating in April. Average journey times for rural pupils fell by eleven minutes each way. The district stopped funding the late bus on Fridays because so few pupils used it. Cycle storage was added at four primary schools after a parents' survey. ## Outcomes

The share of pupils reaching the expected standard in reading rose to 78 percent. Results in writing were flat, and the district has asked schools to review marking. Graduation from the upper school stayed at 88 percent for the third year running. The gap between pupils eligible for free meals and their peers narrowed by two points.
