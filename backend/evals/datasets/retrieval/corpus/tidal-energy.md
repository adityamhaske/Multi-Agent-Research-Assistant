# Tidal stream generation

Tidal stream turbines sit in fast-moving water and work much as wind turbines do, with two
differences that dominate the engineering. Water is roughly eight hundred times denser
than air, so useful power arrives at far lower flow speeds; and the flow reverses twice a
day, so blades must either pitch through or the whole nacelle must yaw.

Predictability is the advantage that distinguishes tidal from every other renewable
resource. Output is a function of celestial mechanics, so a schedule can be published years
ahead, which is worth more to a grid operator than the raw energy figure suggests.

Biofouling and cavitation are the two failure modes that decide maintenance intervals.
