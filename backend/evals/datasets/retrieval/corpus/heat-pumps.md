# Heat pump coefficient of performance

A heat pump moves heat rather than producing it, so it delivers more thermal energy than
the electrical energy it consumes. The ratio is the coefficient of performance, and it
falls as the temperature difference the pump works across rises — which is precisely when
heat is most needed.

Sizing is therefore a design decision about the coldest expected days rather than the
average. Oversizing costs money and causes short cycling; undersizing means resistive
backup runs on exactly the days the grid is most stressed.

Ground-source installations hold a steadier source temperature and so a steadier
coefficient, at a capital cost most retrofits cannot justify.
