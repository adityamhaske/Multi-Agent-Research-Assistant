# Geothermal generation

Geothermal plants draw heat from rock rather than from fuel. A binary-cycle plant passes
hot brine through a heat exchanger to boil a working fluid with a lower boiling point,
which drives the turbine, while the brine is reinjected to sustain reservoir pressure.

Capacity factors are the attraction: output does not vary with weather or time of day, so
a geothermal plant behaves like baseload in a way no wind or solar site can. The
constraint is geography. Economic resources cluster along plate boundaries, and drilling
risk is borne before anyone knows whether a field will produce.

Induced seismicity has stalled several enhanced-geothermal projects, because stimulating
fractures in hot dry rock is difficult to do without also stimulating small earthquakes.
