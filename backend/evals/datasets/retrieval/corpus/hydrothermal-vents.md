# Chemosynthesis at hydrothermal vents

Communities on the deep ocean floor live entirely beyond the reach of sunlight. Where
superheated fluid escapes through cracks in the seabed it carries dissolved hydrogen
sulfide, and bacteria oxidise that sulfide to fix carbon. This is chemosynthesis: the
energy comes from chemistry rather than from light, and it supports a food web of tube
worms, blind shrimp and pale crabs found nowhere else.

The tube worms have no mouth and no gut. They house symbiotic bacteria in a specialised
organ and live on what those bacteria produce, which is why they can grow to two metres in
water that never sees a photon of surface light.

Vent fields are short-lived on geological timescales. When the flow stops the community
dies, so the animals depend on larvae dispersing far enough to reach the next active site.
