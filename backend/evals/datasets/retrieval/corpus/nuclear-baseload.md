# Nuclear output profile

A pressurised-water reactor is thermally efficient at steady output and poorly suited to
following demand, which is why nuclear plants are operated flat and other assets are moved
around them. Fuel cost is a small fraction of the total, so the economics reward running
continuously rather than saving fuel.

Refuelling outages are scheduled years ahead and take weeks, so system planners treat the
loss as known rather than random. That predictability is what lets a grid carry a large
nuclear share without proportionate reserve.

Load following is technically possible and is done routinely in some fleets, but it
accelerates wear on control components and is rarely worth it where cheaper flexibility
exists.
