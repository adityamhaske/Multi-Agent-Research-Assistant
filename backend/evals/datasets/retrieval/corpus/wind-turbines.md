# Wind turbine output

A horizontal-axis wind turbine extracts kinetic energy from moving air. Power available in
the wind rises with the cube of wind speed, so a site with modestly better wind is worth
far more than the difference in average speed suggests. Betz's limit caps the fraction any
rotor can extract at just under sixty percent, and real machines fall short of it.

Turbines have grown because swept area rises with the square of blade length while tower
and foundation costs rise more slowly. Offshore machines are larger still, since transport
by sea removes the road constraints that limit blade length on land.

Output is variable rather than unpredictable: forecasts hours ahead are good enough for
grid operators to schedule around, which is a different problem from intermittency.
