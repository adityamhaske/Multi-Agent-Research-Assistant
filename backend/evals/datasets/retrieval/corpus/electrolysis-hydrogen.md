# Electrolytic hydrogen

Electrolysis splits water using electricity, and its efficiency is bounded by the energy
content of the hydrogen produced against the electricity consumed. Around seventy percent
is typical for current stacks, so hydrogen used to make electricity again returns less than
half of what went in.

That round-trip loss is why hydrogen is argued for where electrification is hard rather
than as storage. Steel reduction, fertiliser and long-haul shipping have no obvious battery
route; a car does.

Stack degradation depends on how the load is cycled. Running an electrolyser only when
electricity is cheap is exactly the duty cycle that shortens its life.
