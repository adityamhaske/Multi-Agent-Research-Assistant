## What is frozen

- **Decisions D-1 to D-24** (RFC §4), including the owner's answers to Q1–Q6.
- **The capability definition** (RFC §5) and the two guarantees (§6).
- **The data model and versioning conclusion** (§7): additive only. No CHECK widening, no change to the corpus SQLite unique indexes, no bundle v3. Any of those makes this release 4.0.0 under `RELEASE.md` M0.03 and must be refused in review.
- **The selection lifecycle** (§8) and **the claim-level grounding contract** (§9, including §9.9's budget behaviour).
- **The extraction specification and format tiers** (§10–§11), under the slip rule D-5: a format ships only if its fixtures, cross-host determinism and packaged-binary ingest pass, and otherwise moves to 3.2. Isolation, selection and grounding never slip.
- **Acceptance criteria AC-1 to AC-43, release gates G-1 to G-6 with their recorded-evidence rule, and the release blockers** (§14).
- **The delivery plan** (§19), with its no-silent-expansion rule.

## Left open by design

- The G-5 numbers X, Y, Z and L (§14.3). The owner sets them from the PR-19 measurements before the release candidate. Until then, the checking cost is an open release risk.

## Amendments

An amendment is a dated section here naming the RFC section it changes, why, and who decided it.

### A-1 (2026-10-29): G-6 reference labels by an LLM judge, not a person

- **Changes:** RFC §17, "Labelling is done by a person against the snippet". The 425 claims
  of the G-6 set are labelled by **Claude Sonnet 4.5, through OmniRoute's Kiro connection
  (`custom:kiro/claude-sonnet-4.5`)**, as a judge (`evals/grounding_judge.py`), with the
  person's rubric from the labelling page as its instructions.
- **Decided by:** the owner, 2026-10-29: "I do not want to label 425 manually - use haiku
  5.5 to do this, use LLM as judge". Neither route to Haiku 5.5 answered on this machine
  (`github/` refused the client with a 466; the `cline/` connection's authentication had
  expired). The owner then chose "Use Kiro Haiku 4.5", and then "use any cheap and best
  model". On a 30-claim trial against what each claim was written to be, Haiku 4.5 agreed on
  26 and Sonnet 4.5 on 25. But Haiku called 3 unsupported claims supported, against Sonnet's 1, and that
  permissive direction is the one that would hide false accepts, so Sonnet 4.5 labels the
  set. Sonnet 5 is not in Kiro's catalog. The checker's `auto/best-fast` alias was served
  that day by `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` and `claude-sonnet-4-6` (OmniRoute's call log): not
  the judge, but a Claude Sonnet too, which a reader of the result should know.
- **What it changes in what G-6 means:** G-6 now measures **agreement between the shipped
  checker and Sonnet 4.5**, not accuracy against a person. False-accept is "the checker
  called supported what the judge did not". Every result built on these labels carries the
  labeller block (`labels.json`), and the release notes say so.
- **What stays:**
  - the judge reads the claim and its snippets only (blind to `authoring.json` and to every
    checker verdict);
  - it may not be the checker under test (`grounding_benchmark` refuses that run);
  - what served each call is recorded from the gateway's headers;
  - an unparseable reply leaves the claim unlabelled, never labelled;
  - the D-23 thresholds are unchanged.

### A-2 (2026-10-30): a decomposed claim's span is measured by the code, from a quote

- **Why:** G-6 run 1 (`eval-2026-10-30-custom-auto-best-fast-grounding-run1.json`) was
  invalid: 157 of 425 claims (36.9%) unmeasured, every one `decomposition_invalid`. The
  checking model, asked for character offsets (RFC §9.2), miscounted them: spans starting
  mid-word, or ending before the date they were meant to cover. G-5's two failed runs had
  the same cause.
- **What changes:** `critic.claim_decompose` returns each claim's `quote`, the sentence's
  own words as one unbroken stretch, instead of `span`. `grounding.locate_quote` finds the
  quote in the sentence and the span is that location. A quote the sentence does not
  contain makes the decomposition invalid, so an invented clause still cannot be placed.
- **What stays:** every §9.2 structural check, unchanged and still in code. Spans lie inside
  the sentence and do not overlap; together they cover every factual token; `cites` name
  only the sentence's own markers. A failure is unmeasured, never supported (AC-35).
- **Measured:** on G-6's first 40 claims, unmeasured fell from 15 to 6. The rest are mostly
  bracketed asides that the model quotes around, which is refused. A full G-6 rerun is owed.

### A-3 (2026-10-30): §9 checks every asserted word and redoes derived figures

- **Why:** reading G-6 run 1 claim by claim found three holes in the checker:
  - **Unchecked asides.** Coverage (§9.2) counted only digits and capitalised words, so
    "(the largest in the region)" could sit outside every quote and never be checked. 39
    of the 40 parenthetical claims were unmeasured, so this had never been measured.
  - **Wrong subjects.** The verifier saw only the model's restatement of a claim, and
    accepted figures stated for another subject: 4 of 34 `wrong_subject` claims.
  - **Derived figures.** A total or difference computed from the snippets' figures was
    rejected before any model saw it, because only the decomposer, which never sees the
    snippets, could state a calculation.
- **What changes:**
  - **Coverage.** Every content word must lie inside some quote. Only joining words may
    sit between quotes, and a negation, cause or sequence never may.
  - **The verifier's input.** `critic.claim_verify` also sees the report's own words for
    each claim, and must find the claim's subject stated for its figure.
  - **Derived figures.** A claim whose figure the snippets lack goes to the verifier,
    which may propose the calculation. It counts only when
    `quantities.calculation_holds` redoes it from operands the snippets state.
  - **Units.** `quantities` carries a converted unit's precision as a step, which fixes
    "6 hours" failing against "6 hours".
- **What stays:** every §9.2 and §9.3 check, each now at least as strict; no overlap
  between quotes (AC-35); the model never decides what the code can check.
- **Measured:** on G-6's first 40 claims, no false accepts (0 of 22), contradicted recall
  1/8 → 8/8, unmeasured 6 → 4. G-6 run 2, over all 425 claims
  (`eval-2026-10-30-custom-auto-best-fast-grounding-run2.json`):
  - unmeasured 13 (3.1%), so the run is valid;
  - false accept 2 of 272 (0.7%, Wilson upper bound 2.6%), down from run 1's 3.6%;
  - contradicted recall 86%, up from 33%;
  - false reject 23 of 140 (16.4%), over the 15% limit. Re-running four of those claims
    on the same code passed three, so most of the gap is run-to-run variance under the
    router alias. The fourth was a defect, addressed in A-4.

### A-4 (2026-11-01): a derived figure may be written without its unit; energy units

- **Why:** among run 2's false rejects, the verifier proposed `subtract 31 19 = 12` for a
  12-point rise between 31% and 19%. The code refused it because `31` is not `31%`. A unit
  conversion (412 GWh as 412,000 MWh) and a cause the snippet words differently ("because
  of", a spreadsheet Reason column) were rejected too. Reading the quantities module for
  this also found that energy units were not parsed at all, so "412 MWh" was compared as a
  bare 412 and matched "412 GWh": a latent false accept.
- **What changes:**
  - In a proposed calculation, a bare operand stands for a percentage only when the
    snippets state exactly that percentage.
  - Add and subtract refuse operands of different kinds.
  - Wh to TWh, spelled out or abbreviated, convert within their own dimension.
  - `critic.claim_verify` is told that a cause given in other words is stated, and that an
    exact unit conversion is the same figure.
- **What stays:** an operand must still be stated in the snippets, and the arithmetic is
  still redone by the code. Every A-3 check is unchanged.
- **Measured:** G-6 on all 425 claims (`eval-2026-11-02-custom-auto-best-fast-grounding-run1.json`,
  the third full run overall). **It meets D-23**:
  - unmeasured 19 (4.5%);
  - false accept 5 of 272 (1.9%), with a Wilson upper bound of 4.3%;
  - false reject 7 of 136 (5.1%);
  - contradicted recall 70%, down from 86%. The 34 contradicted claims it missed were all
    still rejected, as not supported; none was accepted.
  - The multi-source slice holds only 2 negatives, and both were accepted (a subject detail
    in each). That is reported as a slice, not a rate.

### A-5 (2026-11-01): the second routing is a pinned OmniRoute model, not a local one

- **Decision (owner, 2026-11-01):** "use omniroute always instead of local LLM".
- **What changes:** §17 and G-5 asked for the default routing and **one local routing**.
  The second routing is now a pinned OmniRoute model (an explicit `provider/model`, not a
  router alias), so what answered is one model and can be named. No local model is
  benchmarked for 3.1.0.
- **Consequences:**
  - D-24 still applies. Every local route, Ollama included, is labelled
    "checker accuracy on this model: not measured", and runs on it are not refused.
  - The corpus embedder stays local (Ollama `nomic-embed-text`). It is not a language
    model, and corpus mode refuses a remote embedder (§6.1).
  - A preflight on 20 claims had already shown local 7B and 14B models missing every
    threshold, with 10–20% of claims unmeasured, so those runs would have been invalid.
- **What stays:** the default routing (`custom:auto/best-fast`) must meet D-23 for 3.1.0
  to ship (§14.2, blocker 6).

### A-6 (2026-11-01): the desktop is published for macOS only

- **Decision (owner, 2026-11-01):** "forget Windows and Linux versions from now on, I only have
  macOS". Chosen form: a macOS-only 3.1.0. Only the `.dmg` is published, and Windows and
  Linux users are offered the newest earlier build.
- **Why:** a release here is launched and verified on a Mac, and on nothing else. An
  installer nobody launched is not offered.
- **What changes:**
  - **G-1 and G-3:** required for macOS (arm64) only. The Windows and Linux CI evidence
    already recorded stays as history.
  - **G-2:** the macOS pass on a launched build only. WebView2 and WebKitGTK are out of
    scope.
  - **Release mechanics:** the release job publishes only the `.dmg`, and the audit's
    `installers_for` expects only it. The download page gives Windows and Linux their
    newest earlier build and says why.
  - **CI:** Windows and Linux still build, as required checks, so the code keeps compiling
    there.
- **What stays:** every AC on the server, and every desktop AC on macOS. Bundle
  compatibility (G-4) and the benchmark (G-6) are not platform-specific.
